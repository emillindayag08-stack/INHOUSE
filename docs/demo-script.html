The live case
01

File a complaint

Complainant ~2 min
/login → /complaints/new
  1. Log in as the Complainant.
  2. Open File a Complaint. Fill in the respondent's name, department, and whether they're a student or personnel.
  3. Write a short incident description — a sentence is enough for the demo.
  4. Upload the notarized complaint document, then submit.
Say Every case starts with a sworn, notarized complaint. The system gives the complainant a 3-day window to get it notarized — if that window lapses with nothing on file, the Committee can dismiss the case for non-compliance automatically. It's not just a form, it's enforcing the actual procedure.
02

Secretariat intake

Secretariat ~1.5 min
Log out → log in as Secretariat → /cases
  1. Open the new case from the Cases list.
  2. Confirm the notarized document, then click Verify.
Say The Secretariat is the intake gatekeeper — every complaint is logged, timestamped, and checked before it goes anywhere near a hearing.
03

Show-cause order: draft, then approve

Secretariat Chairperson ~2 min
  1. As Secretariat, draft the show-cause order (a line or two of charge details).
  2. Log out, log back in as the Chairperson.
  3. Open the case and click Approve to issue the order.
Say This is a genuine two-person control, not a rubber stamp — the Secretariat drafts it, but nothing reaches the Respondent until the Chairperson signs off. The Chairperson can also send it back for revision instead.
04

Respondent answers

Respondent ~1.5 min
  1. Log out, log in as the Respondent.
  2. Open the case, write a short answer, submit.
Say If the Respondent doesn't answer in time, the process doesn't stall — the Secretariat can proceed without a response once the 5-day window lapses.
05

Prima facie evaluation + risk score

Committee ~2 min
  1. Log out, log in as a Committee member.
  2. Open the case, evaluate for prima facie — mark it Found with a short rationale.
  3. Run the risk assessment and point out the classification it produces.
Say The risk score is a decision-support cue, not an automatic verdict — it flags severity and urgency for the Committee, who can override it with their own judgment call if they disagree.
06

Formal charge, then pathway choice

Secretariat Respondent Complainant ~2 min
  1. As Secretariat, issue the formal charge.
  2. As Respondent, submit a formal answer, then choose Formal Investigation as the pathway.
  3. As Complainant, also choose Formal Investigation.
Say Both sides choose independently, without seeing what the other picked. Agree, and it's final immediately. If they don't agree, the Secretariat has the final call — that's how real cases stay unblocked instead of deadlocking on a disagreement.
07

Hearings — with required minutes

Secretariat ~3 min
  1. Schedule the Pre-Trial hearing (any date/time works).
  2. Try clicking End Formal Investigation right away — point out it's blocked.
  3. Attach the Pre-Trial minutes, then schedule a Trial hearing and attach its minutes too.
  4. Now click End Formal Investigation — it goes through.
Why step 2 matters This is a real server-side rule, not just a UI hint: "Attach the minutes for every hearing before ending the investigation." Skipping the button click won't get around it — the API itself refuses the request.
Say Every hearing — Pre-Trial or Trial — now needs its own minutes on file. It's one of the most recent pieces I added: a real procedural safeguard the old version didn't have.
08

Recommendation, then outcome

Secretariat ~2 min
  1. Attach the recommendation document, then click File recommendation — point out the status changes to Recommendation Filed and the recommendation form disappears, replaced by an "awaiting outcome" state.
  2. Click into Record outcome, pick Liable or Not Liable, write a rationale, submit.
  3. Show the case status land on Resolved (or Dismissed).
Say These used to be one combined step. I split them because in the real procedure, the outcome doesn't come back instantly — it's sent out and then comes back later from the University President's office. Filing the recommendation and recording the outcome are genuinely two separate moments in time, so the system treats them that way now.
09

The paper trail

Secretariat ~1 min
  1. Scroll down to the case timeline and slowly scroll through the full history.
Say Every action on this case — from filing to resolution — is logged with who did it and when. Nothing happens off the record.
If time allows
10

Supporting tools tour

Secretariat optional
  1. Calendar — every deadline, hearing, and meeting across all cases in one view. Point out the case filter dropdown, staff-only, for narrowing it down to one case.
  2. Notifications — every role gets notified the moment something needs their attention.
  3. Dashboard analytics — case counts by status, incidents by college, and the monthly filing trend, for CODI staff.
Say Beyond individual cases, the Secretariat and Committee also get a birds-eye view — this is what makes it a case management system, not just a form.
11

The other pathway, already resolved

Secretariat optional — needs a pre-staged case
  1. Open a second, already-resolved case that went through Position Paper instead.
  2. Scroll to show both parties' position papers, the CODI report, and the final decision.
Say Not every case needs a full hearing. For simpler cases, both sides can agree to go straight to a documents-only track instead — position papers, a CODI report, then a decision. Same system, a lighter process.
Wrap-up

Closing line

"That's the full lifecycle — from a student filing a complaint, through every approval gateway the actual procedure requires, to a recorded, appealable outcome. Every rule you saw enforced — the notarization window, the two-person show-cause approval, the required hearing minutes, the two-stage recommendation — is a real safeguard from the process, not something I invented for the demo."
