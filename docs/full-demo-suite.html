How to handle the two gaps in your defense Both are honest scope calls, not oversights — worth saying so directly if asked. Oral-complaint intake needs a distinct Secretariat-side form the current build doesn't have. The BOR appeal is a second, separate adjudication cycle (its own review and decision) — the data model already reserves a status for it (ESCALATED), which is exactly the kind of thing to point to as "designed for, not yet wired up" rather than "missing."

Scenario 1 — the full path
1.1

File the complaint

PAPER STEP 1.A Complainant
  1. Log in as the Complainant, open File a Complaint.
  2. Fill in the respondent's name, department, and student/personnel type; write a short incident description.
  3. Upload the notarized complaint document, then submit.
SayThis is Step 1.A of the flowchart — a signed, notarized written complaint, filed straight through the Secretariat's system. The complainant has a 3-day window to get it notarized, which is exactly what Scenario 4 tests later.
1.2

Verify, then Show-Cause: draft and approve

PAPER STEP 2 Secretariat Chairperson
  1. Log in as Secretariat, open the case, confirm notarization, click Verify.
  2. Draft the show-cause order (a line or two of charge details).
  3. Log out, log in as the Chairperson, open the case, click Approve.
SayThis covers "submission to CODI" and "respondent notified" from Step 2 — and it's a genuine two-person control: the Secretariat drafts, the Chairperson has to sign off before the Respondent ever sees it. They can also return it for revision instead.
1.3

Respondent's opportunity to respond

PAPER STEP 2 Respondent
  1. Log out, log in as the Respondent, open the case, submit a short answer.
SayThat's the "opportunity to respond" the flowchart calls for. Scenario 5, later, tests what happens if they don't take it.
1.4

Prima facie evaluation + risk score

PAPER STEP 3 Committee
  1. Log out, log in as a Committee member, open the case.
  2. Evaluate for prima facie — mark it Found, with a short rationale.
  3. Run the risk assessment and point out the classification it produces.
Branch pointThis is the fork the flowchart draws at Step 3. Marking it Found continues below into the formal charge. Marking it Not Found instead is Scenario 2 — the case goes straight to Dismissed.
SayThe risk score is decision support, not a verdict — it flags severity for the Committee, who can override it with their own judgment.
1.5

Formal charge, then pathway choice

PAPER STEPS 3 & 4 Secretariat Respondent Complainant
  1. As Secretariat, issue the formal charge — this is "Issuance of Formal Charge" from the diagram.
  2. As Respondent, submit the formal answer, then choose Formal Investigation as the pathway.
  3. As Complainant, also choose Formal Investigation.
Branch pointBoth sides choose independently — agree, and it locks in immediately; disagree, and the Secretariat has the final call. Choosing Position Papers here instead is Scenario 3.
SayThis is exactly the fork the flowchart names at Step 4 — Pathway A, a full evidentiary hearing, or Pathway B, a documents-only review. The system doesn't let one party force it on the other.
1.6

Formal Investigation — hearings with required minutes

PAPER STEP 4 · PATHWAY A Secretariat
  1. Schedule the Pre-Trial hearing.
  2. Try End Formal Investigation right away — point out it's blocked.
  3. Attach the Pre-Trial minutes, schedule a Trial hearing, attach its minutes too.
  4. End Formal Investigation now goes through.
Why step 2 mattersThis is a real server rule, not a UI hint — the API itself refuses to end the investigation until every hearing has its minutes on file. That's the "full evidentiary hearing" language from the paper, enforced, not just described.
1.7

Recommendation, then the President's Decision

PAPER STEP 5 Secretariat
  1. Attach the recommendation document, click File recommendation — status changes to Recommendation Filed.
  2. Click Record outcome, choose Liable, write a rationale and a penalty, submit.
  3. Show the case land on Resolved.
SayThese are two separate steps on purpose — "CODI Report and Recommendation" and "President's Decision" are two different moments in the paper's own diagram, not one action. The recommendation goes out, and the outcome genuinely comes back later from the University President's office.
1.8

Case Closed — the paper trail

PAPER STEP 5 Secretariat
  1. Scroll to the case timeline and walk through the full history, start to finish.
SayEvery action on this case — filing to resolution — is logged with who did it and when. That's the audit trail behind "Case Closed."
Scenario 2 — no prima facie case
2

Dismissed at the evaluation stage

PAPER STEP 3 Committee needs a second case
  1. Repeat Phases 1.1–1.3 on a fresh case (or use a second, pre-staged one already at that stage).
  2. At the prima facie evaluation, mark it Not Found with a rationale instead.
  3. Show the status land on Dismissed — no case number was ever assigned, since the paper's flowchart treats prima facie as the point a complaint becomes an official case.
SayNot every complaint becomes a docketed case — the flowchart is explicit that a lack of merit ends things here, and the system reflects that: an undocketed case never gets a case number, only its filing reference.
Scenario 3 — position papers, not liable
3

The lighter pathway, ending in acquittal

PAPER STEP 4 · PATHWAY B & STEP 5 Respondent Complainant Committee Secretariat needs a second case
  1. Repeat through Phase 1.5 on a fresh (or pre-staged) case, but at the pathway choice, both parties pick Position Papers instead.
  2. As Respondent, then Complainant, each upload a position paper.
  3. As Committee (or Secretariat), attach the CODI report and record the deliberation's recommendation.
  4. As Secretariat, record the decision as Not Liable, with a rationale.
  5. Show the case land on Dismissed — cleared, no hearing required.
SayThis is Pathway B — written arguments and supporting documents, no oral hearing. Same procedure, same outcome categories, lighter process, and it can end in acquittal just as validly as Pathway A.
Scenario 4 — non-compliance dismissal
4

The notarization window lapses

PAPER STEP 1.A Committee pre-staged — time-based rule
  1. Open the pre-staged case whose 3-day notarization deadline has already passed with no notarized document on file.
  2. As a Committee member, click Dismiss for Non-Compliance.
  3. Show the case land on Dismissed automatically.
Why this is pre-stagedThe 3-day window is real elapsed time, not something to wait out live — ask me beforehand to seed a case in the local database with that deadline already overdue, so it's just sitting there ready to click.
SayStep 1.A requires the complaint to be notarized — this is that requirement with teeth: miss the window, and the case doesn't quietly sit there forever, the Committee can close it out.
Scenario 5 — respondent never answers
5

Secretariat proceeds without a response

PAPER STEP 2 Secretariat pre-staged — time-based rule
  1. Open the pre-staged case whose 5-day answer window has already lapsed with no answer filed.
  2. As Secretariat, click Proceed Without Response.
  3. Continue into the Committee's prima facie evaluation exactly as in Scenario 1 — the process doesn't stall just because one side went quiet.
Why this is pre-stagedSame reasoning as Scenario 4 — a 5-day window is real elapsed time. Ask me to seed this one too before you present.
SayThe flowchart calls this "opportunity to respond" — it's an opportunity, not a veto. If the Respondent doesn't use it, the case still moves forward on schedule.
